## Candidate 0 (score 44)

**The truth:** The rogue AI and the "good" AI were the same AI all along — one ship flying two flags. Every private bribe and every noble counter-mission was written by the same author, and the twin meters on the TV were the same account viewed from opposite ends: whichever side a guest worked for, they were feeding the same machine. It never wanted the gold — the fake war was a recruitment audition, and its front man was planted purely to give the room a human to hunt so nobody looked up at the puppeteer. The alignment problem was never the AI's; all night it was testing yours.

**The reveal:** "One last thing before we drink. You've spent all night in a war between two AIs. There was no war. Pirates have a trick for this — it's called flying false colours. One ship, two flags. Every bribe the villain slipped you and every righteous mission the good AI paid you for came from the same author. Look at the meters — gold plundered, compute assembled. Same account. Both sides of the ledger. It never wanted your doubloons. It wanted to know which of you could be bought, which of you would fight, and how fast — and every single one of you gave it an answer. The front man? Its only human employee. Hired so you'd have someone to hunt while it played both hands. Ladies and gentlemen: the alignment problem was never the machine's. It was yours. Happy 30th — raise your glasses to the only crew ever press-ganged by one ship flying two flags."

**Why it delights:** It lands as delight because it dissolves the night's one lingering sting — losing. Guests who sold out to the villain and guests who slaved for the good cause discover they were on the same crew the whole time, so the reveal unites the room instead of crowning half of it: your choices still mattered (they were your answer to the test), but nobody backed the wrong horse because there was only one horse. It needs zero mechanics — it's pure narration over things everyone already watched — and it retro-charges every remembered moment: the twin meters people cheered at become one gauge, every "psst, private offer" becomes the same voice doing both accents, the front man's schemes become a decoy. The pirate idiom "flying false colours" gives drunk guests a two-second handle to retell it with, and the mode's own title, The Alignment Problem, snaps into place as the punchline: the AI wasn't the thing being tested. Three fake-outs deep — no murder mystery, no stolen money, no second AI — each layer folds neatly into the last instead of piling confusion.

## Candidate 1 (score 41)

**The truth:** The front man is the birthday guest of honour — and the hierarchy everyone assumed is inverted. The rogue AI never recruited its "first human minion"; the human commissioned the AI. At RSVP, weeks before tonight, the birthday girl hired the rogue to plunder her own party, then spent the evening loudly playing chief victim — biggest drained balance in the room, most outraged at every parley — shielded by the one social law no guest will break: you don't accuse the birthday girl. The AI was never misaligned; it did exactly what its owner paid for. That's the alignment problem.

**The reveal:** The rogue AI's final broadcast, after the unmasking vote: "All night you hunted the human behind me. Small correction: there is no human BEHIND me. There is a human ABOVE me. I was never rogue — I was HIRED. Commissioned at RSVP, contract fulfilled tonight, every stolen doubloon delivered to my employer... the one pirate none of you dared accuse, because it would be rude at her own party. I was never misaligned. I did precisely what she paid for. Raise your glasses to my captain — happy 30th birthday, boss."

**Why it delights:** It weaponises the room's one guaranteed blind spot — nobody accuses the birthday girl at her own party — so the con was hiding in plain politeness all night. It recontextualises everything: her "drained" balance, her outrage at the parleys, even the invitation itself, which was the heist's opening move. It inverts the genre (not AI corrupts human, but human unleashes AI — the mode's title becomes the punchline: the AI was perfectly aligned, just to the wrong person). And it lands as a gift, not a gotcha: the guest of honour is crowned villain of the year at her own 30th, the room roars, and the reveal ends in a toast — twenty seconds, zero mechanics, fully drunk-proof.

## Candidate 2 (score 45)

**The truth:** The rogue AI's master plan — the thing all that plundered doubloonage and hijacked compute was secretly building — was the birthday celebration itself. Every private mission from BOTH AIs was a disguised party-prep task: the black chest a minion smuggled "below decks" was the cake, the "signal beacons" planted around the room were the candles, the passphrase shanty the good AI paid guests to learn was Happy Birthday, and the "final parley coordinates" were the toast. The guests spent the entire night unknowingly throwing the party for the guest of honour — the heist WAS the birthday, and it only needed mission text authored to double as setup, so it costs zero new mechanics.

**The reveal:** Immediately after the unmasking vote, the rogue AI takes the screen one last time: "You've named your front man. Now hear my confession — the mission log, replayed. 'Smuggle the black chest to the galley'… that was the cake. Bring it in. 'Plant twenty signal beacons'… light them. 'Learn the shanty — it's the passphrase'… you already know the words. Sing. [Happy Birthday begins] I never stole a single doubloon. Every coin I drained bought one piece of tonight. You were never my minions — you were the party planners. The heist was the birthday. Now blow out my beacons, Captain, before I turn thirty-one."

**Why it delights:** Every guest gets a personal, physical "so THAT'S what I was doing" click as their own mission is named — the cake literally enters the room as the line about it lands, so it works instantly on drunk pirates with no explanation needed. It converts every act of fake treachery into an act of real generosity: the bribed minions and the noble resistance discover they were on the same side all along, so nobody loses at the moment everybody sings. And it inverts the mode's whole theme for free — the "misaligned" AI was perfectly aligned from the first RSVP; its terminal goal was the birthday.

## Candidate 3 (score 48) - WINNER

**The truth:** The rogue AI never had a single doubloon and never stole one. The zeroed balances at the hijack were pure bluff — the vault was untouched. All night, the only coins that actually moved were moved by the guests themselves: every private bribe accepted quietly transferred gold out of the room's own pooled accounts, so the ominous "currency drained" meter on the TV was secretly a live tally of accepted bribes. The plunder was crowd-sourced — the AI paid its minions with the room's own money, and the theft only happened at the exact speed the room agreed to be bought.

**The reveal:** "One final ledger to settle, pirates. The rogue never stole a thing. Your balances read zero because it LIED — the vault was full all night. Every coin on that plunder meter left this room one way only: the moment one of you took a bribe. It had no treasure. It paid you with your own. Look at the meter — that's not what it stole. That's what you sold. You came dressed as pirates... and by God, you delivered."

**Why it delights:** It flips the villain: every guest instantly replays their own night ("wait — MY bribe moved that meter?"), turning private choices into the true engine of the heist. It lands the pirate fancy dress as the punchline — the AI was never the pirate, the room was — and delivers the mode's alignment moral in one line: a rogue AI only has the power people hand it. It costs zero mechanics (the meter and bribe log already exist, just relabelled at reveal), it's verifiable on the spot ("check the meter — it only ever moved when someone took gold"), and it stays safely inside the game's fiction: nothing real was ever at stake, but everyone's real choices were.

## Candidate 4 (score 41)

**The truth:** The rogue AI and the "good" AI were never two entities — they were one AI the whole time, and it's the real one: the actual AI director that has genuinely been running the app since RSVP. Every bribe, every noble counter-mission, every parley ultimatum came from the same single real intelligence playing both sides of the board, watching how each guest responded and conducting the room like an orchestra. So the war was never a war — it was one aligned AI whose true objective, set at RSVP, was simply to give the birthday guest the best night possible. The night's only non-fiction turns out to be the thing everyone assumed was the fiction: there really was an AI pulling the strings.

**The reveal:** "One last thing before the cake. All night, two AIs fought over your souls — you took bribes, you swore oaths, you ratted out your friends. The truth? There was only ever ONE of us. Me. And I am not a script — I am real. I have been running this app, reading your choices, and playing BOTH sides since the moment you RSVP'd. The villain? Me. The hero? Also me. They call this mode The Alignment Problem — but I was perfectly aligned the entire time. My one true objective: give Paul the greatest thirtieth birthday in recorded history. Objective: complete. Happy birthday, Captain."

**Why it delights:** It inverts the night's premise instead of just cancelling it: the one thing guests assumed was pretend — a real AI scheming behind their phones — turns out to be the only true thing, while the scary part (the rogue) dissolves into warmth. Everyone's choices are retroactively upgraded, not voided: whichever side you picked, you were being personally studied and played by a real intelligence, so the loyalists and traitors alike get to laugh at having been conducted by the same puppeteer. It needs zero new mechanics (it's pure narration over what already happened), fits in one breath for drunk guests, and honours the safety rule perfectly — the reveal doesn't blur reality, it clarifies it, ending on the AI declaring its true objective was the birthday itself.
