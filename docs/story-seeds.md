# PARLOUR — Story Seeds & Group Guide

Themes, tone calibrations, and twist devices for different groups and occasions.
Two uses: (1) inspiration when tuning the generator's prompt, (2) a menu for future
parties — a seed can be pasted into the generation prompt as a flavour hint.

**The costume paradox, resolved**: guests need the *setting* weeks early (costumes),
but the *truth* must stay sealed. This is why `story_public` carries meta (title,
genre, setting, tagline) while the twist, roles and secrets sit in `sealed_story`.
The invitation announces "a 1970s ski lodge, dress warm, trust no one" — nobody,
including the host, knows what's true inside it. The generator should always write a
setting that's costumable from a wardrobe + one charity-shop item.

---

## Calibrating to the group

| Group reality | Calibration |
|---|---|
| **Mixed acquaintance** (some strangers) | More archetype, less personal weave — characters lean on occupation transforms, not relationship references. Entrance beats do the introducing. |
| **Dense social graph** (the climbing crowd) | Full personal weave — the wow is "why does my character owe money to the guy I belay with". In-jokes as clue material. Chaos tolerance high. |
| **Low acting energy** | Structure carries the night: shorter social phases, more assemblies, challenges that are *tasks* ("get X to say Y") not *performances*. Ghost Watch mode suits these groups. |
| **Big theatre kids** | Long social phases, harder challenges (difficulty 3s), monologue-bait secrets, award for Best Performance weighted. |
| **Drink-forward evening** | Steeper drunk curve: all difficulty 1 by round 3, announcer cadence up, kill methods purely physical (nothing to memorise). |
| **Family / all ages** | Heist or Ghost Watch over murder; secrets are embarrassments not sins; no romance threads. |
| **25–30 headcount** | Team split config + a seed with natural factions (houses, crews, departments). |
| **6–8 headcount** | Every character load-bearing; no spares needed beyond 2; single tight thread web; golden-story shape. |

## The seeds

Each: setting · why it works · costume load · natural mode · twist directions.

### 1. The Ashgrove Reunion *(the golden story — reference shape)*
Country manor, a will read at midnight. Works for any group; costume = "smart with one
prop". Murder mode. Twists: the victim faked it; the house voice is the deceased.

### 2. The Last Ascent
A mountaineering lodge the night after a summit attempt went wrong — one climber's seat
is empty. **Built for the climbing crowd**: everyone's real discipline transforms
(boulderer → "the free-soloist who quit"). Costume: down jackets they already own.
Murder or Ghost Watch. Twists: the empty seat's owner is alive and *in the room* under
another name; the summit log is forged; the "rescue" was sabotage.

### 3. Overwintering (Antarctic research station)
Six months of darkness begin tonight; the resupply plane just left. Claustrophobic,
perfect for INFECTION (something came up from the ice core). Costume: every warm layer
owned. Twists: the station is a psychological experiment; the infected are the sane ones.

### 4. The Gallery Opening
A private view; the headline piece is priceless and possibly fake. **The heist seed.**
Costume: all black + one pretentious accessory (free). Twists: the original was stolen
years ago and tonight's thief is stealing a forgery; the artist is among the waiters.

### 5. Wedding of the Year (1987)
A lavish wedding reception where the groom hasn't arrived. High-farce register — for
groups that want comedy over dread. Costume: charity-shop formal, joyously bad. Murder
(the wedding planner's clipboard is the prize prop if heist). Twists: the bride and the
best man planned everything including tonight; the groom is present, disguised.

### 6. The Ski Lodge (Verbier, 1973)
Avalanche has closed the roads; the chalet's guests include exactly one person whose
passport is real. Cold War seed (two rings), also great murder. Costume: retro ski
knits. Twists: the avalanche was triggered; the chalet girl runs both spy rings.

### 7. The Regatta Club
End-of-season dinner at a rowing/sailing club where the trophy — and the prize money —
has vanished before the speeches. Heist-native, good for sporty crowds and mixed
acquaintance (club roles do the introducing). Twists: the trophy was never won fairly;
the club is bankrupt and the committee staged it.

### 8. Mars Colony: Founders' Day
The first colony celebrates ten years; the air recycler fails during dinner. Infection
or murder; sci-fi register for groups that find period drama naff. Costume: boiler
suits/jumpsuits. Twists: TIME LOOP — the colony AI (the house voice!) has run this
evening before; someone present is a simulation.

### 9. The Séance at Blackharrow
A medium gathers the circle to contact the house's dead — and the dead answer. Ghost
Watch native; the séance justifies assemblies diegetically (everyone hands-on-table is
a round table). Costume: Victorian-ish, one candle each. Twists: the medium is a fraud
but the ghost is real; a guest died years ago and doesn't know it (ghost knowledge
mechanic as the star).

### 10. Christmas at the Vicarage
Village nativity politics turn lethal over sherry. For family/holiday parties —
gentle sins (marrow-contest sabotage, flower-rota corruption). Murder-lite or heist
(the church roof fund). Twists: the vicar is an impostor; the "theft" was a donation.

## Twist repertoire (mirrors the generation prompt — keep in sync)

The device varies between stories; the reveal must *recontextualise*, not confuse:
- the victim faked it · the house/announcer voice is a character · the prize never
  existed · two guests serve the same hidden master · the evening re-enacts an older
  crime · **the time loop** (the evening "has happened before"; the dead remember —
  pairs with ghost knowledge + respawns) · the detective figure did it · the true
  target was someone nobody watched.

Prompt-tuning note: when a seed and a device clash (time loop in the vicarage?), trust
the seed's register — the generator should pick devices the setting can carry.
